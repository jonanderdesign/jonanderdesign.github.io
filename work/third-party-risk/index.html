Third-Party Risk.

A risk model designed so each client’s policy can run in the product, instead of in spreadsheets that were hard to audit.

Overview

Company
Riskallay, an early-stage third-party risk platform
Team
One engineer
When
2024
Result
A risk model that runs each client’s policy in the product, so analysts only review what needs a person

Screens redrawn with invented data.

The problem

Clients used the platform to assess their suppliers and partners through questionnaires. However, each large client had its own risk policy, with far more variables than the platform could handle, so the actual risk calculation happened in spreadsheets, outside the product.

Policies the product could not represent. Each policy lived in a weighted, multi-factor spreadsheet of its own, which was easy to get wrong, hard to audit and a compliance risk in itself.

Every decision depended on an analyst. After the calculation, an analyst still had to review each result manually before any decision could move forward.

The decisions

Decision 1: Two matrix models.

Looking at how clients actually applied their policies, I reduced them to two ways of calculating risk. In a weighted matrix, the client selects the fields and assigns each one a weight; the risk is the weighted sum. In a combined matrix, the product generates every combination of the selected fields and the client assigns a specific risk to each one (for example, a particular country combined with a particular revenue gives risk X).

Decision 2: A routing layer.

Which matrix applies depends on the type of third party: suppliers go through one and partners through another. This allows a single account to manage several policies without leaving the product.

Decision 3: Rules for the clear cases.

Rules based on the score (“if the risk is above 4, deny”) or on exact values (“if the country is Spain, approve”) resolve the clear cases automatically. Analysts only review the ones that need a person.

Result

Each client’s policy runs in the product.

It becomes product configuration (two matrices, a routing layer and a set of rules) instead of a spreadsheet of its own, and it can be audited where it runs. For current accounts that ends the dependence on spreadsheets; for large ones it turns what had been a product limitation into a selling point.
