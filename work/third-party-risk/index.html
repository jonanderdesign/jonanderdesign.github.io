Third-Party Risk.

A risk model designed so each client’s policy can run in the product, instead of in spreadsheets that were hard to audit.

Overview

Company
Riskallay, an early-stage third-party risk platform
When
2024
Result
A design validated to remove the clients’ dependency on spreadsheets

Under NDA: some details are slightly altered, and no numbers are shared.

The problem

Clients used the platform to assess their suppliers and partners with questionnaires. Large clients each had their own risk policy, with many more variables than the platform could handle, so they calculated risk in spreadsheets, outside the product.

Those weighted, multi-factor sheets were easy to get wrong and hard to audit (a risk for regulatory compliance). And every decision waited for an analyst to review the result by hand.

The key idea

By analysing how clients used the platform, I reduced every enterprise case to two ways of calculating risk. A weighted matrix scores each field by its impact and adds the scores. A combined matrix lists every combination of the chosen fields (such as country and revenue), and the client gives each combination its own risk. A routing layer picks the matrix for each kind of third party: suppliers go through one, partners through another.

Rules on the risk score, or on exact field values, then settle the clear-cut cases, so analysts only review the ones that need a person.

  3. Routing layerPicks the matrix for that kind of third party
  4. →
  5. Supplier matrixWeighted or combined

  6. →
  7. RulesApprove, deny or leave for an analyst

Result

The design was validated as the way to remove the clients’ dependency on spreadsheets.

Policies would live in the product, where they can be audited, instead of in each client’s spreadsheets.
